---
title: 'Your own Discord app'
description: "Give a bot its own name, picture, and slash commands by connecting a Discord application of your own"
---

Every bot runs on either Camel's app or a Discord app of your own. Camel's app
needs no setup. Your own app gives the bot its own name, picture, profile, and
top-level slash commands. What the bot can do is the same either way, and you
can switch a bot between them at any time.

## Camel's app or your own

| | Camel's app | Your own app |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Who replies** | Every reply comes from camel, Camel's shared bot | Replies come from your app, with its own name and picture |
| **Slash commands** | Start with your bot's name, like `/mochi roll`, so several Camel bots can share a server | Just the command, like `/roll`, as with any other Discord bot |
| **Talking to it** | People start a message with its name, or reply to one of its messages | People @mention it, or reply to one of its messages |
| **Its profile** | camel's profile, the same for every Camel bot | Its own picture, About me, and status |
| **Direct messages** | People message camel, which passes the message to the Camel bot they used last | People message your app, and only your bot answers |
| **Adding it to a server** | camel joins a server once. After that, you pick a channel for each bot | Your app joins each server you add it to, with your approval |
| **Sharing a server** | Camel bots in one server all reply as camel, so each needs a different name there | It's its own member, so its name and commands never clash with Camel bots |
| **Setup and upkeep** | Nothing to set up or look after | About 5 minutes on Discord's developer site. If you reset its token there, you paste the new one in camelBot |

## Connect your own app

You connect an app in one of three places:

- When you make a bot, choose **Your own app**. The next step is **Connect
  your Discord app**.
- For a bot you have, open its page, then **Settings**. Next to **Discord
  app**, select **Change**, then **Connect another app**.
- In the chat, ask camel-builder to use your own Discord app. It shows a card
  with a token field.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create an application">
    In the [Discord Developer
    Portal](https://discord.com/developers/applications), click **New
    Application** and name it. The name is what your bot is called in Discord.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Copy its bot token">
    Open **Bot** in the side menu, click **Reset Token**, then **Copy**.
    Discord shows a token only once, so Reset Token makes a new one and the
    old one stops working. It may ask for your password or 2FA code.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Paste it in camelBot">
    Paste the token into the token field. It's checked with Discord as soon as
    you paste it. Once Discord accepts it, camelBot shows **Found** and your
    app's name, and sets up the rest of the app for you.
  </Step>
</Steps>

When you're making a new bot, the last step, **Name your bot**, shows the
app's name and picture as Discord has them. Changes there update the app in
Discord too. Select **Create** followed by the name to finish.

An app you connect stays connected even if you stop partway. Next time, pick it
under **Or use one you connected before**.

### What the check may ask you to change

- **Requires OAuth2 Code Grant is on.** Adding the app to a server won't work
  until you switch it off on the app's Bot page. Then select **paste its token
  again**.
- **Message Content is off.** If your bot reads ordinary messages, camelBot
  offers **Turn it on**. Without it, your app only reads messages that mention
  it, reply to it, or are DMs.
- **The Interactions Endpoint URL points somewhere else.** Connecting points
  it at camelBot instead, so the app's commands come to camelBot. Select
  **Connect anyway**, then paste the token again.

## Your token stays private

Under the token field, camelBot says "Goes straight to Camel, not into the
chat. camel-builder never sees it." When camel-builder needs a token, to
connect an app or replace an old one, it shows a card with that same field.
Once Discord accepts the token, the card says "Token saved privately.
camel-builder never saw it."

<Warning>
  Don't paste a bot token into the chat's message box. It's sent like any
  other message, and the chat keeps it. If you do, reset the token in the
  Developer Portal and paste the new one into the token field.
</Warning>

## Up to 5 apps

Each account can connect up to 5 Discord apps, and one app runs one bot. To
free one up, open any bot's **Settings** and select **Change** next to its
Discord app. Pick an app that no bot uses, select **Disconnect** followed by
its name, then **Disconnect** again to confirm. Its commands are cleared, and
you can connect it again later with its token.

## Switch a bot to another app

Open the bot's **Settings** and select **Change** next to **Discord app**. Pick
Camel's app or one of your apps, or **Connect another app**, then select
**Continue**. camelBot lists what will change before anything does:

- On your own app, the bot takes the app's name and follows it from then on.
  Back on Camel's app, it keeps its name.
- Replies come from the new app.
- Commands change, such as from `/mochi roll` to `/roll`.
- The bot keeps its channels. If the new app isn't in one of those servers
  yet, you add it next.
- If it was on one of your apps, that app stays connected, free for another
  bot.

Select **Switch to** followed by the app's name. If the new app isn't in all of
the bot's servers, select **Add to server** for each one.

## Name, picture, and profile

On your own app, **Name** and **Picture** in the bot's **Settings** change the
app in Discord too. Discord allows about two name changes an hour. **More
profile options** has **About me** and **Custom status**. Select **Save to
Discord** to update them.

People can @mention your bot or reply to its messages. With Message Content
on, they can also start a message with its name followed by a comma or colon,
such as `Pebble, roll`.

## Add to Discord in one screen

With your app's client secret, **Add to Discord** is one Discord screen that
picks the server and the channel, as on Camel's app. It's optional. Without it,
you pick the channel in camelBot.

To turn it on, open the bot's **Settings** and find **Add to Discord in one
screen**:

1. Open your app's OAuth2 page from the link there. Under **Redirects**, add
   the address camelBot shows, and save.
2. Copy the app's **Client Secret**. **Reset Secret** shows a new one if you
   need it. Paste it in camelBot and select **Turn on**.

Without the client secret, **Add to Discord** opens a dialog. Choose a server.
If your app isn't in it yet, select the button that adds it, such as **Add
Pebble to Dice Goblins**. Discord opens in a new tab, and the dialog updates
once you approve it there. Then choose a channel.

## When Discord stops accepting the token

If you reset the token in the Developer Portal, the bot stops answering and its
status says **Needs a new token**. A notice on its page says it keeps its
channels. Select **Paste a new token** and paste a new token from the app's Bot
page.

In **Settings**, the **⋯** next to your app also has **Paste a new token**,
**Update from Discord** to read the app's name and picture again, and **Open
Discord's developer site**.

<Note>
Discord requires bots in more than 100 servers to be verified, and approved
for Message Content. On your own app, you go through that with Discord
yourself. Camel's app takes care of it for you.
</Note>
