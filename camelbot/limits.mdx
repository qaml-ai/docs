## Permissions

When you add a bot to a server, Discord asks you to approve these basic permissions:

- View Channel
- Send Messages
- Send Messages in Threads
- Embed Links
- Read Message History
- Add Reactions

On Camel's app, it also asks for **Change Nickname**, so it can use your bot's
name in the server. A bot asks for more permissions only when it uses the
matching features:

| Feature | Additional permissions |
| --- | --- |
| Creating and managing channels and threads | Manage Channels, Manage Threads, Create Public Threads, Create Private Threads |
| Moderation | Kick Members, Ban Members, Timeout Members |
| Giving, taking, or creating roles, and changing access to channels the bot made | Manage Roles |

A bot never asks for Administrator. For moderation and role changes, place
the bot's highest role above the members and roles it manages in **Server
Settings > Roles**. It can't act on the server owner, or give or take
@everyone or a role managed by another app.

To add a bot to a server, you need the **Manage Server** permission there.
`/camel add` and `/camel remove` need **Manage Server** or **Manage Channels**.

## Which messages a bot sees

Every bot gets its own slash commands, button and menu presses, and form
answers, wherever people use them. Messages from other bots and webhooks are
ignored. Which ordinary messages it sees depends on its app.

### On Camel's app

- **In the channels you add it to**, and the threads in them, it sees every
  message.
- **Anywhere else in those servers**, it sees messages that start with its
  name, such as `mochi, roll` or `mochi roll`, and replies to its messages.
- **A message that @mentions camel** goes to the channel's only bot, or to
  the server's only bot when no bot is in that channel.
- **Direct messages to camel** go to the bot whose message you reply to, or
  else to the bot you last used a command or button of.

### On your own app

- **Direct messages** to your app always reach your bot.
- **Messages that @mention it or reply to it** reach it anywhere in its
  servers.
- **With Message Content on**, it also sees every message in its channels and
  their threads, and messages that start with its name followed by a comma or
  colon, such as `Pebble, roll`.
- **With Message Content off**, other messages in its channels reach it
  without their text. When your bot reads ordinary messages, its **Settings**
  offer **Turn it on**.

## What a bot can do

A bot can post in its channels, reply, edit and delete its own messages, add
reactions, and attach files. Ask camel-builder for the features you need:

- **Moderation.** Time out, kick, ban, and unban members, with reasons in the
  server's audit log and the bot's logs. A ban can optionally delete up to
  seven days of that user's recent messages. By default, it deletes none.
- **Roles.** Give and take roles below the bot's highest role, and create
  roles. It can edit or delete only roles it created.
- **Tickets and channels.** Create text channels and threads, including
  private tickets for a member and staff. It can rename, archive, reopen,
  and lock threads in its channels, and delete channels or threads it made.
  Changing access to a channel it made also needs role-management permissions.
- **Message history.** Read earlier messages from its channels when built to
  use history, for example to save a ticket transcript.
- **Reactions.** Respond when people add or remove reactions in its channels
  or on its messages, such as giving a verification role.
- **Member arrivals and departures.** Run a welcome or departure workflow
  when Discord's Server Members intent is enabled for the app.

### Member events need Server Members

On your own Discord app, camel-builder can offer to enable **Server Members
Intent**. You approve it in a card because it lets the app see who is in its
servers. Availability depends on Discord's intent approval. If Discord
requires a review, complete it through the Developer Portal. See Discord's
[current privileged intent review guide](https://github.com/discord/discord-api-docs/blob/main/developers/gateway/getting-started-with-privileged-intent-review.mdx).

On Camel's app, join and leave events work only while Discord allows the
shared app to use Server Members. You can't turn that setting on for the
shared app yourself. If it isn't available, switch the bot to your own app
and enable it there.

## What a bot can't do

- Delete individual messages written by other people, pin messages, or edit
  other people's messages. A ban's optional recent-message deletion is the
  exception described above.
- Change other members' nicknames, act on members at or above its role, or grant
  Administrator, Manage Server, or Manage Webhooks.
- Change or delete existing channels and roles it didn't create. It can
  manage threads in its channels as described above.
- Start a direct message with someone. It can answer direct messages.
- Post outside the channels it was added to or made, and their threads,
  except to answer where it was used.
- Mention @everyone, @here, or a role.
- Work in forum or voice channels.
- Reach private networks. Its web requests go to public `https` addresses
  only.
- Have a website or dashboard of its own.

## Limits

| Limit | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Commands | Up to 25 per bot, each with up to 25 options. An option offers up to 25 choices |
| Command and option names | 1 to 32 lowercase letters, numbers, `-` or `_`. Descriptions up to 100 characters |
| Forms | Up to 5 fields per form, including text, menus, member and role pickers, checkboxes, and file uploads |
| Bot names on Camel's app | 1 to 32 lowercase letters, numbers, `-` or `_`. Not `camel`, `help` or `settings`. Each of your bots has its own name, and two bots on Camel's app in one server can't share a name |
| Names on your own app | 2 to 32 characters. Discord allows about two name changes an hour |
| Files a bot sends | 10 per message, 20 MiB each and 24 MiB in all, as png, jpg, jpeg, gif, webp, txt, md, csv, json, pdf, mp3, ogg, wav or mp4 |
| Files you send in the test channel | 10 per message, 20 MiB each |
| Moderation and role actions | 30 per minute per bot |
| Timeouts | Up to 28 days; the bot can also lift a timeout |
| Messages deleted with a ban | Up to the previous 7 days for the banned user; none by default |
| Channels the bot creates | Up to 200 undeleted channels per server |
| Roles the bot creates | Up to 50 undeleted roles per server |
| Channel history | Up to 100 messages per request |
| Saved data | 100 MB per bot |
| Repeating schedules | 25 per bot, at most once a minute |
| One-time reminders | 200 waiting at once, from 1 second to a year ahead |
| Web requests | Public `https` addresses only, 10 seconds and 1 MB each |
| Messages to camel-builder | 8,000 characters each, and up to 20 in 10 minutes |
| Discord apps | 5 per account. One app runs one bot |

Schedule times are in UTC. When you ask for a post at a set time, tell
camel-builder your time zone.

A schedule or reminder that was due while camelBot was unavailable runs as
soon as it's back. A repeating schedule runs once for all the times it missed.
Schedules don't run while a bot is paused.

## AI allowances

camelBot is free while in beta. AI draws on two daily allowances, and both
reset at midnight UTC.

- **Each bot's allowance** covers the AI a live bot uses in Discord, such as
  chat replies, images, and voice transcription. Once it's used up, the bot's
  AI stops working until it resets. Its commands and features that don't use
  AI keep working.
- **Your camel-builder allowance** covers camel-builder's own work and AI in
  your test channels. **AI today** in the test channel's header shows how much
  is used. Once it's used up, camel-builder takes no new messages and AI in
  tests stops working until it resets.
